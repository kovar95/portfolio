Short answer: in 2026 a small business website typically costs **$800–$4,000 with a freelancer** and **$2,000–$10,000 with an agency**, according to an [analysis of 600+ project quotes](https://projectcostestimator.com/website-cost). A one-page landing page runs $300–$1,500, and an online store starts around $2,500. On top of that, budget **15–20% of the build cost every year** for hosting, maintenance and licences.

Where you hire matters almost as much as what you build. The same analysis puts Eastern European rates at roughly half of Western European ones. Below is what you get at each price, what actually drives the cost, and what to ask before you sign.

## What drives the price

1. **Pages and content.** Five pages and thirty pages are different jobs.
2. **Template or custom design.** A bought theme is faster and cheaper. Custom design costs more, but the site looks like your brand rather than a thousand others on the same theme.
3. **Features.** A contact form is trivial. Online payments, bookings, user accounts and accounting or ERP integrations move the price the most.
4. **Languages.** Every language version is more content and more testing.
5. **Copy and photos.** If you don't have them, someone has to write or shoot them.
6. **SEO and speed.** Basic search setup should be included. Ongoing ranking work is a separate, usually monthly, service.

## The running costs people forget

- **Domain and SSL:** about $15–$50 a year.
- **Hosting:** $5–$50 a month for a typical WordPress site. Watch out for "free first year" offers — the bill arrives in year two.
- **Maintenance:** $50–$500 a month, depending on how much changes.
- **Licences:** premium WordPress plugins run $200–$1,000 a year; Shopify plans are $39–$399 a month before apps.
- **Business email:** $6–$30 per user per month.

## Freelancer or agency?

Agencies bill roughly **2.2× freelancer rates** for the same scope. You pay for a team — designer, developer, SEO — and project management. A freelancer gives you direct contact with the person building your site and a lower price. For a small business site, an experienced freelancer is usually enough. For a large store with integrations, an agency makes sense.

Whoever you choose, ask:

- Will the domain and the site be registered in my name?
- Who makes changes after launch, and what does that cost?
- How fast does the site load on a phone? Ask for a [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) score.
- Can I see two or three sites you've built?

## FAQ

**How long does it take to build a website?**
A simple business site usually takes from a few days to three weeks. The most common delay is content — copy and images from the client.

**Can I build a website for free?**
Yes, with Wix and similar builders. But you pay in time, limits and usually a monthly subscription once you want your own domain. For a serious business, a site you actually own is worth it.

**Do I need a website if I have Instagram?**
Instagram is great for reach, but you don't control it, and it doesn't show up when someone searches Google for "your service + your city". A website is the one place nobody can switch off or change the rules on overnight.

**Is a cheaper website always worse?**
No. An expensive site that is slow and hard to maintain is a worse investment than a simple one that loads fast and brings in enquiries. Compare what you get, not just the price.
