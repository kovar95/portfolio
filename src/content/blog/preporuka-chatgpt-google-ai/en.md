Short answer: ChatGPT and Google AI don't pick businesses at random. They recommend the ones with clear, consistent, verifiable information across the web — a complete Google Business Profile, a steady stream of reviews, a website that plainly says what you do and where, and mentions in directories and on other sites. There is no secret AI hack. It's good local SEO, done properly.

Why now? Google's [AI Mode](https://support.google.com/websearch/answer/16011537) is rolling out across dozens of countries and languages, and more searches end with a ready-made answer instead of ten links. An [Ahrefs study of 300,000 keywords](https://www.shopifreaks.com/google-ai-overviews-now-reduce-top-ranking-pages-clickthrough-rates-by-58-according-to-new-ahrefs-research-analyzing-300000-keywords/) found that the top organic result gets about **58% fewer clicks** when an AI Overview appears above it. If you're not in the answer, a share of your customers will never see you.

## How does ChatGPT decide which business to recommend?

Ask ChatGPT "recommend a plumber in Lyon" and it searches the web, then usually shows a list of businesses with ratings and review counts. A [Suff Digital study](https://www.suffdigital.com/resources/data-studies/chatgpt-local-business-cards) of 3,160 local questions (September 2026) found that list in **95.1%** of answers, with around a dozen businesses each.

A smaller [study by LinkTech Solutions](https://linktechsolutions.io/insights/how-chatgpt-picks-local-businesses) (20 searches, September 2026) is more useful in practice:

- **85% of the sources** ChatGPT cited were directories and review sites. Only 2 of 53 were the businesses' own websites.
- **80.7%** of listed businesses were in the exact city the user asked about.
- Businesses at the top had more reviews (median 112 vs 72), yet the most-reviewed business ranked first in only 2 of 19 answers.

Both studies looked at US cities, and both authors sell SEO services, so treat the numbers as direction, not law. The pattern still holds: **AI trusts what others say about you**, not only what you say about yourself. ChatGPT's local business data comes mostly from [Mapbox plus other providers, including Bing](https://www.searchengineworld.com/getting-your-business-on-the-map-in-chatgpt-local-search-comes-to-chatgpt).

## What about Google AI Overviews and AI Mode?

Google is refreshingly blunt here. Its [official guidance for site owners](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features) says there are **no additional requirements or special optimizations** for AI Overviews or AI Mode. A page needs to be indexed and eligible to show in Search. You don't need special schema markup or new "AI files".

What Google does recommend is the basics: don't block crawling in robots.txt or at your host or CDN, keep important content in text, offer a good page experience, and keep your Business Profile and Merchant Center information up to date. If Google already understands your business, its AI very likely does too.

## What should a small business actually do?

| Step | Why it helps | Rough effort |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Complete your Google Business Profile | Google AI and Maps read it directly | 1–2 hours |
| Same name, address and phone everywhere | AI cross-checks sources; mismatches cost trust | 1–2 hours |
| Ask for reviews continuously | ratings and counts appear next to recommendations | ongoing |
| Get listed in relevant directories | AI cites directories far more than business sites | half a day |
| Say what, where, for whom and how much on your site | AI quotes specific sentences | 1–2 days |
| Fast, indexed website | no indexing, no AI visibility | depends |

### 1. Treat your Google Business Profile as the foundation

Fill in everything: the most accurate primary category, address or service area, opening hours (including holidays), phone, website, services with short descriptions, and real photos. Post an update now and then. Reply to reviews, including the bad ones, calmly and specifically.

### 2. Keep your details identical everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number should match across your website, Google, Facebook, Instagram and every directory. If you moved across town and three listings still show the old address, AI can't tell which one to believe.

### 3. Get reviews steadily, not in bursts

Send every happy customer a direct review link when the job is done. Ten reviews spread across a year look more trustworthy than thirty in one week. Never buy reviews: platforms remove them and customers spot them.

### 4. Show up where AI looks

Since AI leans heavily on directories and review sites, be present on the ones that matter in your market and industry: Yelp or Tripadvisor where they're used, local chamber or trade association listings, booking platforms, and "best in town" roundups from local media and blogs. Because ChatGPT partly relies on Bing, a free **Bing Places** listing is worth ten minutes — you can import it from your Google profile.

### 5. Write a website that answers questions

AI loves sentences it can repeat as an answer. Instead of "We offer quality services at competitive prices", write something like: "We service air conditioners across Lisbon; a call-out costs €40 and you'll usually get a slot within two days." Give each core service its own page, and add an FAQ and price ranges — people search for them and AI happily quotes them. (Planning a new site? Here's [what a small business website costs in 2026](/blog/koliko-kosta-izrada-sajta/).)

Here's what that looks like in practice. Picture a nutritionist in Vienna whose homepage says:

> "Welcome! I help you become the best version of yourself with a personal approach and expert guidance."

It sounds nice, but an AI can't tell from it where she works, what she offers or what it costs. The same message, written so AI can quote it:

> "Nutritionist in Vienna (3rd district) and online. I create personal meal plans for weight loss, athletes and pregnancy. The first consultation, including a body composition analysis, takes 60 minutes and costs €90; follow-ups are every two weeks."

The second version answers exactly what people ask ChatGPT: "nutritionist Vienna price", "nutritionist for athletes", "online nutritionist". Going through rewrites like this with clients, it's been the cheapest step with the biggest payoff — no code, just clear sentences.

### 6. Get the technical basics right

Check in Google Search Console that your pages are indexed. Make sure the site loads fast on mobile. Some hosts and CDNs offer one-click AI bot blocking — double-check you haven't shut out Googlebot or OAI-SearchBot (the crawler ChatGPT uses for search). `LocalBusiness` structured data isn't required for AI features, but it helps search engines read your address, hours and contact details correctly.

## How can you check whether AI already recommends you?

Ask like a customer would: "Recommend a [service] in [city]" — in ChatGPT with search on and in Google AI Mode. Try a few phrasings and a private window. Note which businesses appear and which sources the AI cites: that's your list of places to be. Repeat monthly, because answers shift.

## FAQ

**Do I have to pay to be recommended by ChatGPT or Google AI?**
No. Organic recommendations can't be bought. You only pay with time — or someone's time — to fix your profile, site and directory listings.

**How fast will I see results?**
Google Business Profile changes often show up within days. Reviews, directory listings and website content take a few weeks to a couple of months.

**Do I need a website if I have a Google profile and Instagram?**
Yes. A profile is a great start, but your website is the one place where you control what's said about your services, prices and service area — and both Google and AI use it to confirm everything else.

**Is this just SEO with a new name?**
Mostly. Google says there are no special rules for AI features. The difference is emphasis: reviews, directories and clear, quotable answers matter even more.
